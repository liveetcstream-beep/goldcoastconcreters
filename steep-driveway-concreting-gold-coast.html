⚡ Essential Rules for Steep Concreting on the Gold Coast

  • Maximum Council Slope Limits: Under City of Gold Coast planning schemes and AS 2890.1, the maximum allowable domestic driveway gradient is generally 1 in 4 (25%). Anything steeper than 1 in 5 (20%) requires engineered transition zones.
  • Preventing Car Scraping (Transition Curves): A sudden change in slope causes vehicles to scrape their front spoilers or exhaust pipes. We build 2.0m–2.5m curved transition aprons with a gentler 1-in-8 (12.5%) grade at both the street kerb and garage entry.
  • The Right Concrete Slump: Pouring concrete on a steep hill requires a stiff 60mm–80mm slump mix. If the concrete is too wet, it will slump down the hill under gravity, leaving thin crests and pooled valleys.
  • High-Traction Non-Slip Finishes: Smooth trowel or stamped finishes should NEVER be used on slopes. We apply aggressive mechanical broom-drag textures, transverse grooving, or coarse 10mm–14mm exposed aggregate with non-slip silica sealers.
Finishing Options

The Best Non-Slip Concrete Finishes for Steep Hills

1. Heavy Broom-Drag Finish

Drawn across the concrete perpendicular to vehicle travel, creating continuous parallel micro-ridges that provide maximum tire mechanical grip even during subtropical deluge downpours.

2. Coarse Exposed River Stone Aggregate

Natural 10mm–14mm river gravels washed to an assertive depth. Combines stunning natural beauty with high friction, ideal for prestige acreage properties in Mudgeeraba and Tallai.

3. Transverse Saw-Cut Grooves (Tine Finish)

On extreme slopes (exceeding 18%), transverse grooves are formed into the surface every 50mm, creating positive mechanical interlock for vehicle tires and commercial delivery vans.
