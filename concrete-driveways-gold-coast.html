Local Engineering

How We Engineer Driveways for Gold Coast Soil & Weather

1. Reactive Clay Soils (Pimpama 4209 & Ormeau 4208)

The northern Gold Coast corridor has heavy black clay soil (Class H1/H2). Rain causes it to expand significantly. We excavate 150mm deep, lay 100mm compacted roadbase, and use SL72 steel mesh to absorb ground heave movement.

2. Coastal Salt Air Protection (Southport 4215 & Hope Island 4212)

Salt spray near the Broadwater corrodes unsealed concrete and internal steel rebar over time. We pour high-density N32 concrete mixes with low water ratios and seal with marine-grade acrylic sealers.

3. Steep Slope Runoff (Pacific Pines 4211 & Highland Park)

Sloped driveways require specialized concrete slump control (80mm slump mix) so concrete doesn't slump down the hill during placement, combined with heavy broom or exposed aggregate anti-slip finishes.

Common Questions

Gold Coast Concrete Driveways FAQ

How thick should a residential concrete driveway be on the Gold Coast?

Under Australian Standard AS 3727.1-2016, standard passenger vehicle driveways must be a minimum of 100mm thick with SL72 steel mesh laid on 50mm bar chairs over a 100mm compacted roadbase. For heavy 4WDs, caravans, or steep sloped sites in Pacific Pines, we pour 125mm–150mm thickness with N32 strength concrete.

Do I need Gold Coast City Council (GCCC) permit for a driveway crossover?

Yes. The vehicular crossover (VXO)—the concrete section connecting your property boundary to the kerb—requires GCCC approval. It must meet GCCC Standard Drawings (150mm thickness, N25 concrete, invert kerb layback cut). We assist with crossover applications and Form 16 certification.

How long before I can drive my car on a new concrete driveway?

You can walk on your new driveway after 24 to 48 hours. Light passenger vehicles (cars, small SUVs) can park on it after 7 days (when concrete reaches ~70% design strength). Heavy trucks, boats, or caravans should wait 28 days for full 100% cure strength.

Why do driveways crack in clay soil areas like Pimpama and Coomera?

Pimpama (4209), Coomera (4209), and Ormeau (4208) feature reactive Class H1/H2 clay soil. Wet summer storms cause clay swelling, while hot dry spells cause shrinkage. Without a compacted 100mm roadbase sub-base, mesh elevated on chairs, and saw-cut expansion joints spaced every 3m-4m, the slab will crack under soil tension.
