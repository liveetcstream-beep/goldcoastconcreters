⚡ Quick Summary: Concrete MPa & Slump Rules

  • What Is MPa? Megapascal (MPa) measures the compressive crushing resistance of concrete after a standard 28-day curing period (1 MPa = ~145 psi).
  • Standard Gold Coast Ratings:
    • N20 (20 MPa): Footpaths, garden shed slabs, and non-structural domestic walkways.
    • N25 (25 MPa): Standard residential car driveways and patio slabs (AS 3727.1).
    • N32 (32 MPa): Marine coastal zones (within 1km of surf beaches), heavy machinery sheds, horse float parking, and exposed aggregate driveways.
    • N40 (40 MPa): Commercial truck loading docks, high-traffic commercial aprons, and industrial warehouse floors.
  • What Is a Slump Test? It measures the consistency, flowability, and water content of fresh concrete using a standard 300mm steel cone per AS 1012.3.1. Standard driveway mix is ordered at 80mm–100mm slump.
  • The Danger of Adding Water on Site: Adding garden hose water to make concrete easier to push destroys its water-cement ratio, reducing compressive strength by up to 30% and causing surface dusting and shrinkage cracking.

How the Slump Test Works on Site (AS 1012.3.1)

When a concrete mixer truck arrives from Boral or River Sands, a slump test is performed to confirm that the batch meets workability specifications before discharge. The test uses a hollow truncated steel cone (300mm high, 200mm base, 100mm top). Concrete is placed in three layers, rodded 25 times per layer with a steel tamping rod, leveled off, and the cone is lifted vertically. The distance the concrete slumps downwards is measured in millimetres.

Low Slump (60mm – 80mm)

Stiff consistency used for steep sloping driveways and kerb extrusions where concrete must hold its form without slumping downhill under gravity.

Standard Slump (80mm – 100mm)

The ideal balance for flat residential driveways, allowing excellent consolidation around steel mesh while maintaining low water-cement ratios.

Pump Slump (100mm – 120mm)

Formulated with water-reducing superplasticizers (not raw water) to allow smooth flow through 40–80 metres of concrete boom and line pump hoses.

Excessive Slump (>140mm Danger)

Indicates too much water. Causes heavy stone aggregates to sink to the bottom of the slab while weak, watery cement paste bleeds to the top, causing surface flaking.
