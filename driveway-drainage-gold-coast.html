⚡ City of Gold Coast Driveway Drainage Laws at a Glance

  • No Uncontrolled Discharge to Neighbours: Under Queensland building codes and local council bylaws, stormwater from your concrete driveway cannot legally be discharged across adjoining private property boundaries.
  • Minimum Fall Standards (AS 3727.1): Driveways must be engineered with a minimum 1 in 100 (1%) cross-fall or longitudinal grade to prevent surface water ponding.
  • Garage Inundation Thresholds: For driveways sloping downward towards a garage (negative grade), council standards require a trafficable linear trench grate connected to a legal point of discharge or a gravity pump well.
  • Crossover Swales & Culverts: If your street frontage features an open grass swale or table drain, the council requires an engineered concrete pipe culvert (typically 375mm diameter) with reinforced headwalls to maintain roadside stormwater flow.
Drainage Systems

The 4 Most Common Driveway Drainage Solutions on the Gold Coast

1. Polymer Concrete Trench Grates (Heelguard)

Installed flush across the driveway in front of the garage door. Features heavy-duty Class B (car) or Class C (truck) galvanized steel or stainless heelguard grates that collect high-velocity sheet runoff.

2. Cast In-Situ Concrete Spoon Drains

A dished concrete channel (typically 300mm–600mm wide and 50mm deep) formed directly into the slab. Ideal for long acreage driveways in Mudgeeraba and Nerang to divert hillside water to natural gullies.

3. Inverted Crown & Cross-Fall Grading

Shaping the concrete surface during the pour with a 2% slope towards one edge or a central valley line, directing stormwater away from building foundations without expensive underground pipework.

4. Council Roadside Pipe Culverts & Headwalls

Engineered reinforced concrete pipes installed beneath the crossover in rural and semi-rural suburbs (Ormeau, Willow Vale, Tallai) to allow municipal roadside ditch drainage to pass unobstructed.
