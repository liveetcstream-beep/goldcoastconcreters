Slab Engineering

6 Engineered Concrete Slab Types We Pour

Every concrete slab must be engineered for its specific weight load, ground sub-base, and moisture environment:

1. Residential Shed & Workshop Slabs

100mm–125mm slab depth, 50mm compacted roadbase foundation, SL82 steel mesh on plastic bar chairs, poured at N32 strength with perimeter edge beams for steel portal frames.

2. Heavy Industrial & Warehouse Slabs

125mm–150mm depth, 150mm compacted roadbase, double SL82/SL92 steel mesh, poured at N40 high-strength concrete with helicopter power-trowel finish for heavy truck traffic.

3. House Extension & Granny Flat Foundations

Engineered Class M or H1/H2 raft slabs with 0.2mm plastic vapor barriers, doweled tie-ins to existing house footings, and structural trench beams complying with AS 2870.

4. Outdoor Patio & Alfresco Entertaining Slabs

100mm N25/N32 concrete slabs with broom or decorative finish, designed with 1:100 fall away from house walls to prevent rainwater pooling.

5. Double Garage & Carport Slab Foundations

125mm reinforced N32 concrete slabs with thickened perimeter footings designed to carry heavy 4WDs, boat trailers, and workshop tool chests without cracking.

6. Water Tank & Heavy Equipment Pads

Heavy-duty 150mm N32 reinforced concrete pads with double SL82 steel mesh engineered to support 22,000L+ rainwater tanks and heavy air conditioning plant equipment.

Gold Coast Geotechnical Factors

Soil & Subgrade Ground Analysis Across Gold Coast Suburbs

Pouring a durable concrete slab on the Gold Coast requires adapting to distinct local soil profiles:

Reactive Clay Soils (Pimpama, Coomera & Ormeau)

Clay soils expand dramatically when wet and shrink in dry periods. We prevent slab cracking by laying 0.2mm moisture barriers, compacting 75mm roadbase, and elevating SL82 mesh on bar chairs.

Sloping Acreage & Rock (Kingsholme & Willow Vale)

Acreage blocks off Montego Hills feature steep rock outcrops. We bench cut subgrade, pour deep trench footings into solid rock, and utilize concrete line pumps for steep site access.

Alluvial Industrial Ground (Yatala Enterprise Area)

Subsoil near the Albert River contains fine alluvial clay. Industrial slabs supporting 10-tonne forklift loads require 150mm roadbase sub-base compaction and high-strength N40 concrete mix.

Our Process

How We Engineer, Quote & Pour Your Concrete Slab

Step 1: On-Site Measure & Subgrade Check

We visit your site, measure dimensions, check fall gradients, assess soil class, and provide a transparent fixed-price written proposal with zero hidden fees.

Step 2: Excavation & Compaction

We excavate to specified depth, remove organic topsoil, lay a 50mm–100mm roadbase subgrade, and compact mechanically using a heavy plate compactor.

Step 3: Formwork, Vapor Barrier & SL82 Mesh

We set up precision timber formwork, lay 0.2mm plastic vapor underlay, and position SL82 steel reinforcement mesh raised on plastic chairs per AS 2870.

Step 4: N32/N40 Pour & Power Trowel Finish

We pour high-density N32 or N40 concrete mix, needle vibrate edge footings, power screed to level, and helicopter power trowel or broom finish the surface.

Workmanship Warning

Why Cheap Concrete Slabs Crack (And How We Fix It)

Slab structural failures occur when cheap operators cut corners during subgrade prep:

1. Skipping Mechanical Needle Vibration

Pouring deep footings without mechanical needle vibration leaves air pockets (honeycombing) that drastically weaken load-bearing capacity. We needle-vibrate all trench footings.

2. Omitting Plastic Moisture Underlay

Failing to lay 0.2mm plastic vapor barrier under the slab allows ground moisture to wick into the concrete, causing damp garage floors and rusting internal steel mesh.

3. Steel Mesh Laid Directly On Ground

Placing steel mesh directly on earth means the steel rusts from soil dampness and offers zero tensile strength. We elevate all steel mesh on 50mm plastic chairs.

Frequently Asked Questions — Concrete Slabs

Q: How much does a concrete slab cost on the Gold Coast?

A: Concrete slab costs depend on slab dimensions, thickness (100mm to 150mm), concrete grade (N32 vs N40), steel mesh weight, and excavation access. Because every site is unique, we do not provide fixed online estimates. Call us on 0411 914 157 or fill out our quick quote form for a free, accurate, no-obligation on-site quote.

Q: What thickness concrete slab do I need for a machinery shed?

A: We recommend a minimum 125mm to 150mm slab thickness poured at N32 or N40 strength with SL82 steel mesh on bar chairs for heavy vehicle or machinery storage.

Q: Do I need QBCC licensed concreters for a slab in Queensland?

A: Yes. Any residential concreting work valued over $3,300 AUD in Queensland must be performed by a contractor holding an active QBCC license (#15299707).

Q: How long before I can build on a new concrete slab?

A: Foot traffic is safe after 48 hours. Light shed framing or timber construction can commence after 7 days, while heavy steel building portal frames can be erected after 28 days of concrete curing.

Q: Why is plastic moisture underlay necessary under a concrete slab?

A: The 0.2mm plastic vapor barrier stops ground moisture from rising through porous concrete, preventing damp indoor floors and protecting internal steel mesh from moisture rusting.

Q: What concrete strength is required for house extension slabs?

A: We pour N32 (32MPa) concrete mix with SL82 steel mesh for house extension slabs in accordance with AS 2870 (Residential Slabs and Footings).
